## Abstract
This study examines how managers interpret algorithmic performance systems.

## Introduction
Algorithmic management is spreading. However, we know little about managerial interpretation.

## Method
We conducted 24 semi-structured interviews in a retail bank, using an instrumental case study design. Data were analysed with thematic analysis, supported by a codebook of 14 codes. Sampling was purposive across functions. Findings are displayed in a theme-by-case matrix.

## Results
Managers described the system as "an impartial mirror that removes favouritism from appraisals". Others described it as "a black box that scores us on things we cannot see". A third group described it as "a tool we have learned to game". These three repertoires organize our findings, and the third is retained as a negative case.

## Discussion
The study contributes to the literature on algorithmic management. A limitation is the single case.
